# Who gets what, when

A small service business has few clients, so decide client by client, not in percentage waves.

## The five groups

| Group | Who | What they get | How they hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| New clients | Anyone who hasn't signed yet | The new standard rate, from a set date (usually now) | Website, quotes, proposals |
| Standard existing | Good clients at or near the normal rate | The new rate after notice | Short message, offer to talk |
| Re-scope | Paying far below the rest because the work grew | The real work priced properly, or the work cut back to fit the old fee | Message naming the change in work, then the price |
| Protect | About 25% of revenue or more, or strategically vital | Their own timing, usually at renewal, often two dated steps | Call or meeting first, then written confirmation |
| Release candidates | Clients the owner would be fine to lose (lowest rate, most hassle, poor fit) | The new rate, or a rate the owner is genuinely happy at, with full notice | Message with a clean handover offer |

## Rules for sorting

- **New clients first.** They have no old price to anchor to, and it tests demand. If new clients say yes at the new rate, the owner can move existing clients with more confidence.
- **Draw a line in time.** "From [date], new work is at [rate]." Existing clients move on their own schedule after that.
- **Notice.** Check the contract or engagement letter first. If it lets fees change by notice, give that notice at least. If it fixes the fee until renewal, the change rides on renewal. With no terms, 30 to 60 days is a common courtesy for monthly clients, longer for long-standing or big ones. Present this as judgement.
- **Same work, same price, in the end.** Clients doing similar work should land on the same rate. Long tenure can earn a gentler path (a phased step, a later date), not a permanent discount. Every held or phased rate gets an end date.
- **Big rises can be stepped.** If an existing client is far below the new rate, two steps with dates (for example, half the gap now, the rest in six months) can keep a good client who would balk at one jump. Use it for good clients, not to soften every rise.
- **Protect means talk, not exempt.** The key client still moves, but by conversation, at a natural point (renewal, new scope), and possibly by less or more slowly. Show the owner the cost of keeping them where they are against the risk of losing them.
- **Releasing is fine.** Freed hours at a low rate are worth more refilled at the new rate. Releasing with proper notice, a referral if the owner knows someone suitable, and a clean handover keeps the owner's reputation intact. Never word a release price as a punishment.
- **Options, not a menu.** If a client can't afford the new price, the one alternative is a smaller scope at a lower price that the owner can genuinely deliver. Never the same scope for less.

## Timing

Stagger to lower risk and workload:
1. New-client rate live now (website, quotes).
2. Standard existing and re-scope messages sent together, effective after notice.
3. Release candidates in the same batch or the next, with full notice.
4. Key client: call booked this week or ahead of renewal; their date set in the call.

Avoid announcing during a client's known crunch period if the owner mentions one.
