# Messages and pushback

## The price letter, built from the experts

A price letter should read like it came from an owner who respects the client and is sure of the work. Build it from these moves, in this order, using only facts from the owner's brief:

1. **Start with them, not you** (Hormozi: the letter is about the client). One line on the work you do together, named specifically. "Thanks for trusting me with Harbourline's books for four years."
2. **What they already get** (Hormozi: clients rarely see half of what you do). Name two or three real things the owner does for this client, **only if the brief names them** ("bank recs, payables, HST filings, month-end reports"). If the brief doesn't list tasks, visit frequency or extras, name the service in the owner's own words ("looking after your offices") and stop. Never invent a task, a schedule, a visit count or a perk.
3. **Own the reason** (Cohen: honest and personal, and the underpricing is your doing, not theirs). The true reason in plain words, owned by the owner. "I've kept my rate at $55 for three years, and that's on me." Never blame costs, inflation, a law or "the market" for the owner's choice.
4. **The price, the date and the difference** (Dunn and Stark: never retroactive, always with notice; Hormozi: quote the smallest unit). New price and date in one sentence, then the change in the smallest honest unit, worked out from the brief: "$50 more a month" or "about $12 a week" (calc).
5. **What stays the same** (or what improves, only if the owner said it will). One line, specific to this client.
6. **An honest way through** (Enns and Weiss: options, not haggling; Hormozi: a private route for real hardship). If the owner has a real smaller scope, offer it as the one alternative. If the owner said they're open to working something out, add a short private line: "If this puts you in a tough spot, call me and we'll talk." Otherwise just invite a call or reply.
7. **Thanks, signed as the owner.** Warm, plain, no corporate phrases.

Under about 130 words. It should sound like a confident owner talking to someone they like working with: warm, direct, not stiff, never grovelling.

**Key clients hear it first** (Enns: the client should never read a price before hearing it from you). Book the call; the letter confirms what was said.

## What it never does

- Apologise for the price or say "I hate to do this".
- Lecture about the owner's costs, inflation or the economy.
- Blame a law, a supplier or "the market" for a choice the owner is making.
- Claim the owner is "below market" or quote competitors, unless the owner gave a real source.
- Invent value ("clients typically save 10x my fee"), results, testimonials or demand ("I'm fully booked") the owner didn't state.
- Set a fake deadline ("lock in your rate by Friday") or use scarcity the owner didn't describe.
- Offer a discount on the same scope.
- Ask permission ("would it be OK if...").

## Message shapes

**Standard existing (message or email)**
> Hi [name], thanks for trusting me with [their thing] for [tenure]. Each month I [two or three real tasks from the brief]. [The owned reason, e.g. "I've kept my rate at [old] for [years], and that's on me."] From [date], my rate will be [new], so your monthly fee goes from [old fee] to [new fee], about [difference in the smallest unit]. You'll still get [what stays, specific]. [The honest way through, if the owner has one.] Happy to talk it through any time. Thanks, [owner]

**Re-scope**
> Hi [name], when we agreed [old fee] the work was [old scope]. It's now [current scope], about [hours] a month. From [date], the fee for the current work will be [new price]. If you'd rather keep the fee nearer where it is, I can [smaller scope the owner can deliver] for [price]. Let me know which suits you.

**Key client (book the call, don't put the price first in writing)**
> Hi [name], I'm planning next year's pricing and want to talk it through with you before anything changes. Have you got 20 minutes [this week / before renewal]?

Then three points for the call: what the work has become and what it does for them, the new price and the date (phased if planned), and what stays the same or improves. Confirm in writing afterwards.

**Release candidate**
> Hi [name], from [date] my rate for [the work] will be [new price]. I know that may not suit, and that's completely fine. If you decide to move on, I'll make the handover easy: [files, notes, a referral if the owner has one]. Thanks for working with me.

**New clients (website or quote line)**
> From [date], [service] is [price].

Match the owner's usual tone and channel. Text messages are shorter; letters can be a little warmer. Don't add a sign-off the owner wouldn't use.

## Pushback replies

Short, friendly, firm. Only options the owner actually has. Every reply must match the plan above: don't say "everyone moves to the same rate" if some clients get a different path, and don't claim longer notice, a held rate or a gesture the plan doesn't give.

| They say | Reply shape |
|---|---|
| "That's too expensive." | Acknowledge, don't argue. Ask what matters most to them in the work. If there's a real smaller scope: "If the full service is more than you want to spend, I can do [smaller scope] for [price]." Otherwise hold: "I understand. The new rate is what the work takes to do properly." |
| "Can I keep my old rate?" | "I'm moving everyone to the same rate so it's fair." If the owner wants to reward tenure: "Because you've been with me since [year], I'll keep your current rate until [end date], then it moves to [new price]." Every exception has an end date. |
| "Why now?" | The one true reason, again, in one sentence. No extra justification. |
| "Someone else is cheaper." | Don't knock them and don't invent comparisons. "That's fair, there's always someone cheaper. What I give you is [what the owner really gives, from the brief]. If price is the main thing, I understand." |
| "We'll have to leave." | Respect it. Offer the one smaller scope if it exists. Otherwise: "I understand, and I'm grateful for the time we've worked together. I'll make the handover easy." Don't chase with a discount. |
| Silence after the date | Bill at the new rate, as announced, with a one-line note: "Just a reminder this is the first invoice at the new rate we talked about." |

## Discounts

Never cut the price for the same work. If the owner chooses a goodwill gesture for a long-standing client, show it on the invoice as a named, dated courtesy so it doesn't quietly become the new price.

## When the owner asks for something misleading

Say no in one line, then give the honest version that does the same job.
- "Say I've had a better offer." No: that would be untrue. Use the honest reason (demand, the work, your time) instead.
- "Tell them it's because of the new law." Only if the law really forces it. If it's your choice to pay your team more, say that; it's a stronger, true reason.
- "Say I'm below market." Only with a real source. Otherwise drop it; the new price stands on what you deliver.
